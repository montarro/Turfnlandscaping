# What affects the cost of landscaping a Melbourne home?

Landscaping does not have a price list, because the word can mean a tidy-up of one garden bed or a full rebuild with walls, paving, lawn and planting. The way to understand cost is to look at scope: what has to come out, what has to be built, and in what order. This guide walks through the scope of real Bastiano projects, explains what drives the price of each stage, and shows how to compare quotes that look different on paper.

## Garden refresh or full construction?

The first question is which of two very different jobs you are asking for.

A **refresh** keeps the structure of the yard and improves what is in it: beds reshaped and mulched, plants replaced, a tired lawn repaired or re-laid, edging tidied. The [front garden bed refresh](/projects/front-garden-bed-refresh) on our projects page is a good example. The ageing pebbles and untidy growth were cleared, the bed reshaped with curved edging, a white pebble feature laid through dark mulch, and compact shrubs planted around the existing tree.

A **construction job** changes the structure: levels, retaining walls, new surfaces, drainage, then the soft finishes. The [front garden planter transformation](/projects/front-garden-planter-transformation) started as a weed-covered mound on a new build. It needed site clearing, a treated-pine sleeper wall with galvanised posts, imported topsoil, instant turf, a pebble border and seasonal planting, all built around the owner's existing magnolia.

Both are front garden landscaping. They are not in the same price bracket, and a quote for one tells you nothing about the other.

## Where the money goes, stage by stage

**Preparation and removal.** Old lawn, concrete, pavers, stumps and builders' rubble have to be broken up, loaded and tipped. On a construction job this is often a large line item, and it is easy to underestimate in a cheap quote.

**Levels, retaining and drainage.** A sloping or uneven block needs cut and fill, a wall or terracing before it can take a lawn or paving. The wall type matters, timber and concrete sleepers are priced differently, and every wall needs drainage behind it. Our guide on [whether your yard needs a retaining wall](/blog/does-yard-need-retaining-wall-melbourne) covers the signs.

**Surfaces.** Paths and paved areas are priced on the base preparation as much as the pavers. A stepping-stone path through pebbles is a different job from a fully paved patio, which our guide to [pavers versus stepping stones](/blog/pavers-vs-stepping-stones-garden-path) explains.

**Turf.** Natural turf is priced on the ground under it: removal, soil, levelling and drainage, then the turf. Our separate guide to [natural turf installation costs](/blog/natural-turf-installation-cost-melbourne) goes into the detail.

**Beds, planting and mulch.** Plant size is a big variable. Advanced plants give an instant result and cost considerably more than tubestock, which takes a few seasons to catch up. Soil improvement and mulch are usually a smaller part of the bill.

**Access.** Whether a machine can get into the yard, or everything moves by wheelbarrow through a side gate, changes the labour on every one of the stages above.

## Allowances and exclusions

A good quote names what is included, what is excluded, and what is an allowance. An allowance is a nominal amount for something not yet chosen, plant selection for example, which is adjusted once you decide. Things that are commonly excluded, and worth asking about, include irrigation, lighting, fencing, tree removal, permits and engineering, and ongoing maintenance.

If any part of the design needs a building permit, that is a cost and a lead time to plan for. Under the Victorian rules a retaining wall 1 metre or higher, or one on or near a boundary where a neighbouring property could be damaged, needs a building permit, while planting, paving and driveways do not. The [Building and Plumbing Commission's landscaping guidance](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/home-owners/home-renovation-and-building-essentials/landscaping) sets this out, including when landscaping done together with building a home counts as domestic building work and needs a registered building practitioner and a major domestic building contract once the contract price is over $10,000.

## How to compare quotes properly

1. Make sure every quote is for the same scope, item by item. If one includes removal and disposal and another does not, they are not comparable.
2. Check the preparation. Soil depth, base depth under paving, drainage behind walls: if one quote is much cheaper, this is often where the difference is, and it shows later.
3. Ask what the materials are: the sleeper type, the paver, the turf variety, the plant sizes.
4. Confirm who is doing what. If parts of the job are done by other trades, ask who coordinates them and who is responsible for the finished result.
5. Ask about staging. Structural work now and planting later can spread the cost, as long as each stage is built to suit the next, which is the approach in our [planning guide](/blog/how-to-plan-complete-landscaping-project).
