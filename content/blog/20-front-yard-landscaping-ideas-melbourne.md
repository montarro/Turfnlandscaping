# Front yard landscaping ideas from Bastiano projects

A front yard has two jobs. It has to get people from the street or the driveway to the door easily, and it has to make the house look like it belongs on its block. Everything else, the lawn, the beds, the lighting, works around those two. Rather than another list of ideas pulled from stock photos, this guide looks at front yards we have actually built, what changed on each, and why, so you can see which approach fits your own frontage.

## What the front yard is for

Before choosing plants or materials, answer three questions:

1. **Where do people walk?** From the driveway, from the footpath, from the letterbox. The path should follow that line, not a decorative curve nobody uses.
2. **What should the street see?** The house, usually. Planting that will grow to hide the front windows, or a wall that cuts the house off from the street, rarely improves the look.
3. **How much time will you give it?** A lawn needs mowing and watering. A mulched bed with the right plants needs an hour or two a season. Be honest about this, because it decides most of the layout.

## Idea 1: give a new build a frame

Most new builds are handed over as bare ground between the driveway and the fence. The [front garden planter transformation](/projects/front-garden-planter-transformation) turned a weed-covered mound into a raised treated-pine sleeper planter with galvanised posts, filled with topsoil and laid with instant turf, finished along the front with a white pebble border and seasonal flowers. The owner's existing magnolia was kept and built around.

**Why it works.** The low wall gives the garden a clean, level edge against the driveway and footpath, which is the thing a bare new-build frontage lacks most. The lawn is a usable size, and the planting is along the edge, where it is seen from the street without hiding the house.

**Upkeep.** A lawn to mow, a bed of seasonal colour to refresh, and timber to re-stain when the owners want to. Our guide to [landscaping a new build after handover](/blog/new-build-landscaping-checklist-melbourne) covers what to check before a job like this starts.

## Idea 2: a path you can see at night

The [instant turf and feature planter with garden lighting](/projects/garden-lighting-turf-planter) project started with patchy lawn, scattered pavers and an ageing pebble strip. The old surfaces came out, new turf went in, stained sleeper planters framed shaped beds of red mulch and white pebble, a stepping-stone path was set through the pebbles to the front door, and low-voltage lights were set along the path and beds. The established roses and the rock feature were kept.

**Why it works.** The path is where people actually walk, it is lit, and the beds are shaped around it rather than the other way round. Contrasting mulch and pebble make the layout read clearly from the street, by day and after dark.

**Upkeep.** Lawn mowing, mulch topped up, and the lights checked occasionally. Pebble beds do not need replanting, which is why they feature in our [low-maintenance landscaping ideas](/blog/low-maintenance-landscaping-ideas-melbourne).

## Idea 3: fix the bed beside the door

Not every frontage needs rebuilding. The [front garden bed refresh](/projects/front-garden-bed-refresh) dealt with one tired pebble bed beside the driveway: the ageing pebbles and untidy growth were cleared, the bed reshaped with curved edging, a white pebble feature laid through dark mulch, and compact shrubs and seasonal plants added around the existing tree, with access to the services kept.

**Why it works.** The entrance is what visitors see first. A defined bed with contrast and tidy edges changes the impression of the whole house for a fraction of the cost of a full rebuild.

**Upkeep.** Minimal: mulch top-ups and occasional pruning. Our guide to [choosing plants and mulch](/blog/choose-plants-and-mulch-melbourne-garden) covers what to put in a bed like this.

## Idea 4: a lawn that is always mown

The [synthetic turf front lawn](/projects/synthetic-turf-front-lawn) replaced the old surface with a compacted, drained base and dense synthetic turf, finished with timber edging and a river-pebble border against the existing established garden.

**Why it works.** For owners who want a green frontage without weekend mowing or summer watering, and whose front lawn is small enough that the base work is affordable, synthetic turf gives a lawn that looks the same every day of the year.

**Upkeep.** Leaves removed, pile brushed up now and then, edges kept clear of garden growth. Whether it suits your frontage depends on sun, use and how you feel about a surface that is not alive, which our [natural versus synthetic comparison](/blog/natural-vs-synthetic-turf-melbourne) goes through.

## Idea 5: level a corner block

The [stained sleeper raised garden](/projects/stained-sleeper-raised-garden) took a sloping corner frontage and held a new, level lawn behind a dark-stained timber sleeper wall with galvanised posts, following both street frontages, with a pebble strip and flowers along the top of the wall.

**Why it works.** A corner block is seen from two streets, and a slope that falls to the footpath wastes the front garden. The wall creates usable lawn and a crisp edge from every angle, and the dark stain ties it to the house.

**Upkeep.** Lawn mowing, flowers refreshed seasonally, and the wall re-stained when the colour fades. If your frontage slopes, our guides on [whether you need a retaining wall](/blog/does-yard-need-retaining-wall-melbourne) and [timber versus concrete sleepers](/blog/timber-vs-concrete-sleeper-retaining-walls) cover the decision.

## Idea 6: a simple lawn across the whole frontage

The [front garden natural turf installation](/projects/natural-turf-installation) started as bare, uneven ground with timber offcuts and broken pavers across it, between the house, a paling fence and a painted picket fence on the street. It was cleared, raked and levelled, and laid with natural turf cut in to the fence lines and path edges.

**Why it works.** Sometimes the best front yard is an even, well-prepared lawn and nothing else. It suits a frontage with existing fences and paths that already give it structure.

**Upkeep.** Mowing and watering, more in the first weeks while the turf establishes, as our guide to [the best time to lay turf](/blog/best-time-to-lay-turf-melbourne) explains.

## Which approach suits your frontage?

- **Bare new build:** frame it (idea 1), then decide between lawn and beds for the middle.
- **Tired established garden:** start with the path and the bed by the door (ideas 2 and 3) before replacing everything.
- **Small frontage, no time for mowing:** synthetic turf or pebble beds (ideas 2 and 4).
- **Sloping or corner block:** level it with a wall first (idea 5); nothing else works until the ground does.
- **Flat block with good fences:** a simple, properly prepared lawn (idea 6).
